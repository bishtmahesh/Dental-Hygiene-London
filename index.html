What is direct access hygiene?

Direct access makes dental hygiene in London simple: you book straight in with a GDC-registered direct access dental hygienist. Your visit starts with a clinical assessment to confirm the planned hygiene treatment is appropriate, followed by your professional clean.

  • No dentist firstNo referral or check-up needed to book.
  • Professional cleanPlaque, calculus and staining removed where appropriate.
  • Not a check-upIf something needs a dentist, your hygienist will explain and help you book.

Please read

Existing fillings, crowns and other dental work

Direct access hygiene is not a full dental examination. Your hygienist may find something that needs further assessment by a dentist.

Fillings, crowns, veneers, bridges, implant restorations, bonded retainers and other dental work can already be loose, damaged or compromised, often without symptoms. Hygiene treatment can sometimes reveal or dislodge this.

Not covered by the hygiene fee

Repair, re-cementation, replacement or investigation of existing dental work. Any examination, X-rays or restorative treatment is booked separately at the relevant dental fee. If you need more time than expected, extra hygiene appointments are charged separately too.

FAQs

Questions about seeing a hygienist

Do I need to see a dentist first?

No. You can book directly with a hygienist. They’ll carry out a clinical assessment first and may recommend a dentist depending on what they find.

Is this a dental check-up?

No. It’s a hygiene appointment and doesn’t replace a full examination by a dentist.

How much does it cost?

The initial appointment is a fixed £[XX]. Examinations, X-rays and any repair or restorative treatment are charged separately at the relevant dental fee.

Can I book just to have stains removed?

Yes, where it’s clinically appropriate. Your hygienist will check it’s suitable for you first.

How often should I see a hygienist?

We recommend a hygienist appointment at least every six months. Regular cleans remove the build-up of plaque and tartar that can lead to gum disease.

What’s the difference between a scale and polish and a deep clean?

A scale and polish is a routine clean above and just below the gum line, for healthy gums or mild inflammation. A deep clean (scaling and root planing) cleans several millimetres into gum pockets under local anaesthetic, for established gum disease. Your hygienist will tell you which you need.

Is it normal for teeth to feel sensitive after a clean?

Yes. Some sensitivity after a clean is common and temporary. Your hygienist can suggest what helps.

How long will my teeth feel clean, and how do I keep them that way?

The effects last several months if you keep up a good routine at home: brush and clean between your teeth twice a day, and cut back on sugar, fizzy and acidic drinks, and smoking. Your hygienist will show you the right techniques and products.

What if a filling or crown comes loose?

Your hygienist will explain what has happened and the next step. Repair, re-cementation or replacement needs a dentist and isn’t included in the hygiene fee. If something already looks weak, they may recommend a dentist checks it before that area is cleaned.

Could I need more than one appointment?

Yes, if your needs are more extensive than expected, for example heavier calculus or inflamed gums. Your hygienist will discuss it with you first, and extra or longer appointments are charged separately.

Will I have photos or a scan?

Where appropriate, yes. Photos or a digital scan help your hygienist show you any area that may need a dentist, but they don’t replace an examination.

I haven’t seen a dentist in years. Can I still book?

Yes. Your hygienist may also recommend a dental examination, as a hygiene appointment doesn’t replace routine check-ups.
